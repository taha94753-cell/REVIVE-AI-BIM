How it works

One project. Six questions a project manager actually asks.

Current delay, measured, not guessed

From the baseline and the latest SPI, REVIVE finds the earned schedule: the day on which the plan had reached the work actually done. The gap to today is the delay to date, confirmed by re-networking the remaining work from the status date.

  • Earned Schedule on your own P6 baseline
  • Activity progress picks up out-of-sequence work
  • Slip per window, split by client, weather and contractor events

Three finish dates, side by side

If the remaining work goes to plan. If the current pace continues. And the machine-learning forecast, trained on Pakistani projects at 25% and 50% of contract time. When they disagree, that is the conversation to have.

  • XGBoost with an 80% range, cross-validated
  • Monte Carlo P50 and P80 finish dates
  • Risk band and a project health score

Every forecast explains itself

TreeSHAP splits the gap between the average project and this one into exact contributions. No black box in front of a client or an examiner.

  • Contributions add up exactly to the forecast
  • What-if sliders re-forecast as you drag
  • Outcome-leaking fields deliberately excluded

The cheapest recovery, by linear programming

Which activities to accelerate, by how many days, so every day bought costs less than a day of liquidated damages. Weather-sensitive work and budget caps included.

  • Time-cost curve, solved point by point
  • Before and after Gantt, finish-date risk
  • Carbon and overheads avoided

A phone on site, a dashboard in the office

Scan a QR code. Tap what happened: late IPCs, material stoppages, variations, weather days. Every open dashboard of that company re-measures, re-forecasts and re-plans within a second.

  • Revocable, unguessable phone links
  • Keeps the update offline, sends when signal returns
  • Observed lost weather days suggested automatically

Optional, but it shows

Connect an IFC model and every column, slab and wall takes the risk of the activity that builds it. The forecast and recovery are written back into the IFC as a property set that opens in Revit and Navisworks.

  • Links by WBS_Code, or by storey and IFC class
  • 4D build sequence: baseline, forecast, recovered
  • Floor area and storeys fill the forecast form

Built for companies

Your projects. Your people. Nobody else's.

01

Company workspaces

Each company signs up once and runs as many projects as it has, each with its own schedule, model and history.

02

Isolated by design

Every record carries its company and every read filters on it. Another company's ids simply do not exist for you, and the tests prove it.

03

Roles

Admins add people and delete projects; members analyse. Passwords are scrypt-hashed and sessions expire.

04

Revocable site links

Phones get an unguessable link, never an account. Lost a phone? Reissue the link and the old one stops working.

05

Hardened

Content Security Policy, CSRF checks, upload type and size limits, login throttling, bounded inputs.

06

Runs on your network

Everything is vendored, so it works offline in a site office or a defence room. Live weather is optional and fails soft.
