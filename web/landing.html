Five questions, answered from the schedule you already have.

Current delay, measured, not guessed

From the baseline and the latest SPI, REVIVE finds the earned schedule: the day on which the plan had reached the work actually done. The gap to today is the delay to date, confirmed by rebuilding the network of the remaining work from the status date.

  • Earned Schedule on your own P6 baseline
  • Activity progress picks up work done out of sequence
  • Slip per window, split by client, weather and contractor events

Three finish dates, side by side

One date assumes the remaining work goes to plan, one assumes the current pace continues, and one comes from the machine learning model trained on Pakistani projects at 25% and 50% of contract time. When they disagree, ask why.

  • Ten questions, an 80% range, tested on unseen projects
  • Monte Carlo P50 and P80 finish dates
  • Risk band and a project health score

Every forecast explains itself

The forecast splits into the average project plus one exact contribution per answer, each with an owner and an action, so you can show a client or an examiner what drives the number.

  • Contributions add up exactly to the forecast
  • Sliders update the forecast as you drag
  • Fields that leak the outcome are left out

The cheapest recovery, by linear programming

REVIVE picks which activities to accelerate and by how many days, so each day bought costs less than a day of liquidated damages. Budget caps and work that depends on weather are part of the problem.

  • Time against cost, solved point by point
  • Before and after Gantt, risk on the finish date
  • Carbon and overheads avoided

A phone on site, a dashboard in the office

The site engineer scans a QR code and taps what happened: late IPCs, material stoppages, variations, weather days. Every open dashboard of that company measures, forecasts and plans again within about a second.

  • Revocable, unguessable phone links
  • Keeps the update offline, sends when signal returns
  • Observed lost weather days suggested automatically

Questions, answered plainly.

Do I need a BIM model?

No. Everything runs on the baseline schedule and site reports. Connect an IFC model if you have one and the forecast also appears on the columns, slabs and walls each activity builds.

Which schedules can I import?

Primavera P6 .xer files, MS Project XML and CSV. You can also start with the labelled sample project to see every sheet with data.

How accurate is the forecast?

The table above comes straight from the trained model card. Every figure is scored on projects the model never saw, and the hardest test leaves a whole company out.

Does the current delay use machine learning?

No. It is measured from the schedule with Earned Schedule and a critical path update, which is why it can act as an independent check on the forecast.

Who can see my projects?

Only your company. Every record carries its company and every read filters on it. Another company asking for your project simply gets “not found”.

Do site engineers need an account?

No. They scan a QR code and send updates from a phone link you can revoke and reissue at any time.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Every library and font is stored with the app, so it runs on your own network. Live weather is optional and never blocks a forecast.

Is any of the data augmented?

Yes. Some records are augmented: scenario rows that are not verified projects. They are labelled wherever they appear. They help train the model, and every score is measured on real completed projects only.
